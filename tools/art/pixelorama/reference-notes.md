# Krsna picture reference notes

Research checked 2026-09-14. These links are visual references for original pixel drawings, not source artwork copied into the game. No reference photographs are bundled here.

## Tulsi / tulasi leaf

**Current art direction:** the game now depicts a manjari flower/bud spike, with
two small leaves at its base, rather than an isolated leaf. NParks describes
small pink or white flowers clustered in pairs along a spike-like inflorescence;
the sprite simplifies these into pale buds on a slender stalk.
[NParks flower description](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/2/2/2275)
The leaf references below remain useful for those basal leaves.

The intended plant is **Ocimum tenuiflorum**, also called holy basil; Kew records both tulsi and tulasi as names. Its leaves vary between elliptic, oblong, and ovate-oblong, with serrated margins and a distinct petiole. They are not a long, smooth-edged spear. [Kew species description](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:453130-1/general-information)

### Photograph references

- **JIRCAS close-up:** [species page](https://www.jircas.go.jp/en/database/thaivege/079), [leaf photograph](https://www.jircas.go.jp/sites/default/files/thaivege/079A_4.jpg). Japan International Research Center for Agricultural Sciences describes elliptic-oblong or ovate leaves, an obtuse or acute tip, a tapered to rounded base, and wavy or serrated margins.
- **Singapore NParks close-up:** [species page](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/2/2/2275), [foliage photograph](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/-/media/ffw/migrated/round2/flora/2275/a52ff88123354ce89aa5d3e763306251.jpg). NParks identifies the broad elliptical shape, slightly toothed margin, hairy surface, and pinnate/net venation.

### Pixel-art interpretation

Use one broad green oval blade, widest around its middle, with a modest pointed tip and a separately readable stem. Give the outline several small teeth, not deep holly-like spikes. A lighter midrib and a few diagonal side-veins should supply most of the interior detail; avoid a single long spear shape or a smooth generic teardrop. These are simplification choices based on the botanical features above, not a claim that all cultivars have identical proportions.

## Pointed ceremonial conch / shankha

The relevant shell is **Turbinella pyrum**, not a generic fan-lipped souvenir conch. The Met identifies its Indian **Śaṅkh**, object 89.4.2504, as a Turbinella pyrum shell trumpet. [Met object record and photograph](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/504901)

Horniman Museum explicitly identifies the same species as the shell used for sankh trumpets in Hindu temples. Its other end-blown specimen description explains that the apex is cut off to form a mouth hole. The long natural tapered end and the blowing opening should not be confused. [Horniman 2005.141](https://www.horniman.ac.uk/object/2005.141/), [Horniman end-blown specimen](https://www.horniman.ac.uk/object/object-218281/)

### Pixel-art interpretation

Prioritize an elongated cream/white, swollen pear-like body tapering at both ends: a short stepped spiral at one end, and a longer slender taper at the other. Keep the visible aperture narrow and attached to the body, rather than a broad pink fan or wing. Two restrained curved shade bands can suggest the whorls without making it a round snail icon. This is the intended silhouette simplification for the game's 24-pixel cell, not a literal copy of the artifacts' decoration or fittings.
